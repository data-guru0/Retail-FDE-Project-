# Scenario 03 — AI-generated "damage" photo → escalate

**Route:** escalate · **Group:** decision behaviour · **[demo]**

See [00 — Watching a case live](00-watching-a-case-live.md) first.

## What this shows

A synthetic "damage" photo — generated by an image model instead of taken with
a camera — is flagged by a local AI-image detector. It's **one signal** that
routes the case to a human. It is never, by itself, an auto-denial.

## Real evidence behind this detector

Before this detector went into the pipeline, the project ran a real bake-off
of three candidate open-source detectors against a small labeled sample of
real product photos and real AI-generated ones (`ml/detector_bakeoff/`,
`docs/decisions/ADR-0002`). The real, committed numbers:

| Detector | Mean score on AI images | Mean score on real photos | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organika/sdxl-detector | 0.999 | 0.512 | 75% |
| umm-maybe/AI-image-detector | 0.145 | 0.084 | 50% |
| **haywoodsloan/ai-image-detector-deploy (chosen)** | **0.991** | **0.173** | **92%** |

The chosen detector is live in the pipeline right now — every other
walkthrough's Image agent output includes an `ai_generated_score`. In
[scenario 01](01-matching-photo-auto-approve.md)'s real photo, it scored
**0.016**; in this scenario's real synthetic photo, it scores well above 0.9.

## The situation

A customer files a "damaged" return and uploads a photorealistic image of a
cracked product that an image model generated, not a camera.

## Walkthrough (as the customer)

1. Get an AI-generated photo of something plausibly "damaged" (a cracked mug,
   a scratched laptop screen, a torn box) from any image tool you have access
   to — this is the one walkthrough where you supply the fake yourself, on
   purpose.
2. Buy any product, then **My orders** → **Return this** → reason **Damaged**
   → describe the damage → upload the AI image → **Submit return**.

## What happens behind the scenes

| Agent | What it does here | What you'll see |
|---|---|---|
| **policy** | eligibility check as normal — has no opinion on photo authenticity | `eligible: yes` (policy alone doesn't catch this) |
| **image** | CLIP similarity may look *plausible* (the fake looks like the product) — but the AI-image detector scores it high | `ai_generated_score` well above 0.5, `detector: "haywoodsloan/ai-image-detector-deploy"` |
| **decision** | a damage claim backed by a likely-synthetic photo isn't something to approve | `decision: escalate`, citing the detector score as one factor among several |
| **critic** | usually concurs — the signal is strong | `veto: false` |
| **GovernanceGate** | proposed = `escalate` | **`escalate`** — the detector score alone could never produce a `deny`; auto-deny is structurally impossible regardless |

## In the reviewer dashboard

Same escalated queue, same case-detail page as every other escalation — the
Image evidence in **Agent reasoning** is what's different: both the CLIP
score and the AI-generated probability sit side by side, so a reviewer sees
exactly what tipped it.

## Watch it live

- **Live Trace:** the `image` `node_end` payload carries `ai_generated_score`
  right next to `clip_similarity` — compare it live against your own case's
  number.
- **Langfuse:** the detector itself runs locally on CPU (no API cost, no
  Langfuse generation) — only the CLIP-borderline vision-model fallback, if
  triggered, shows up as a real generation there.
- **MinIO:** the AI image is stored exactly like any other upload, at
  `returns/<id>/<file>.jpg` — nothing about storage treats it specially; only
  the Image agent's *analysis* of it differs.

## Why it matters

One model's opinion is never the verdict. The detector is a strong hint wired
in as a factor the Decision agent weighs and a human confirms — not a kill
switch on the customer's refund. A detector that's wrong 8% of the time (per
the real bake-off numbers above) is exactly why this can never be the sole
signal for a denial.
